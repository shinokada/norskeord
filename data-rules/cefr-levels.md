# CEFR Level Guide (for vocab/uttrykk classification)

Condensed from the standard CEFR self-assessment grid, merged down to the
levels this app actually uses (`a1 | a2 | b1 | b2 | c` — CEFR's C1/C2 are
combined into a single `c`). Use this to judge which level a new word or
expression belongs at, based on how complex/frequent/abstract it is —
not the full communicative-competence descriptors, just the vocabulary
cues.

## A1

Everyday, concrete, high-frequency words: self, family, immediate
surroundings, shopping, local area. Simple, isolated phrases. No
abstraction.

## A2

Still concrete and personal (routines, work, immediate needs), but
broader: descriptions of background, habits, plans, simple narration.
Vocabulary starts covering everyday objects/situations beyond the
immediate personal sphere.

## B1

Familiar topics handled with some fluency: work, school, leisure,
travel, personal interests. Words start covering feelings, wishes,
experiences, and connected/explanatory language (reasons, plans,
opinions) rather than just facts.

## B2

Abstract and topical vocabulary: current affairs, viewpoints,
advantages/disadvantages, contemporary issues. Words support fluent,
spontaneous, more technical or professional register — regular
interaction with native speakers without strain.

## C

Wide-ranging, precise, and idiomatic vocabulary: nuanced shades of
meaning, specialized/technical terms outside one's own field, literary
and abstract style, idiomatic expressions and colloquialisms. Words a
learner would need to understand or produce complex texts and speech
with near-native precision.
