**Ikke** is not the only word that moves in front of the finite verb in subordinate clauses.

More generally, many **sentence adverbs** (*setningsadverb*) move there.

## Main clause

The sentence adverb comes **after** the finite verb:

> Han **kommer ikke**.

> Han **kommer aldri**.

> Han **kommer kanskje**.

> Han **kommer ofte** for sent.

## Subordinate clause

The sentence adverb comes **before** the finite verb:

> fordi han **ikke kommer**

> fordi han **aldri kommer**

> fordi han **kanskje kommer**

> fordi han **ofte kommer** for sent

---

## Common words that behave like this

### Negation

> ikke

Main clause:

> Han er ikke hjemme.

Subordinate clause:

> fordi han ikke er hjemme

---

### Time/Frequency

> aldri (never)

> alltid (always)

> ofte (often)

> vanligvis (usually)

Main clause:

> Han kommer aldri.

Subordinate clause:

> fordi han aldri kommer

---

### Probability

> kanskje (perhaps)

> sikkert (certainly)

> antakelig (probably)

> muligens (possibly)

Main clause:

> Han kommer kanskje.

Subordinate clause:

> Jeg tror at han kanskje kommer.

---

### Attitude/Evaluation

> heldigvis (fortunately)

> dessverre (unfortunately)

> faktisk (actually)

Main clause:

> Han er faktisk flink.

Subordinate clause:

> Jeg ser at han faktisk er flink.

---

## Compare

### Main clause

> Han **har aldri** vært i Norge.

### Subordinate clause

> Jeg vet at han **aldri har** vært i Norge.

Notice how **aldri** moves in front of the finite verb (**har**).

---

### Main clause

> Hun **kommer kanskje** i morgen.

### Subordinate clause

> Jeg tror at hun **kanskje kommer** i morgen.

---

## A subtle point about ofte

You'll often hear:

> Han kommer ofte for sent.

and

> fordi han ofte kommer for sent

So **ofte** follows the same pattern.

However, adverbs like *ofte* are a bit more flexible than *ikke*, especially in spoken Norwegian.

---

## A useful rule

For Norwegian learners, it's often enough to remember:

### Main clause

> Subject + finite verb + sentence adverb

> Han kommer ikke.

> Han kommer aldri.

> Han kommer kanskje.

### Subordinate clause

> Subject + sentence adverb + finite verb

> at han ikke kommer

> at han aldri kommer

> at han kanskje kommer

This is sometimes called the **leddsetningsregel**:

> In subordinate clauses, sentence adverbs (*ikke, aldri, kanskje, ofte, heldigvis, dessverre,* etc.) are normally placed before the finite verb.
