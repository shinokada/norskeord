In your sentence, **selv** means **“even”**, not “self.”

> **De tok seg tid til å dyrke kjærligheten selv i en travel hverdag.**
> = **They took time to nurture their love even in a busy everyday life.**

### Why does **selv** mean “even”?

Norwegian **selv** has several meanings:

1. **self** → *meg selv* = myself
2. **even** → *selv om* = even though / *selv i...* = even in...
3. **even/including** for emphasis → *Selv han kom.* = Even he came.

Here:

**selv i en travel hverdag**

literally has the idea of:

> **even in a busy everyday life**

The sentence emphasizes that **despite having a busy everyday life**, they still made time for their relationship.

Compare:

* **De tok seg tid til å dyrke kjærligheten i en travel hverdag.**
  = They took time to nurture their love in a busy everyday life.

* **De tok seg tid til å dyrke kjærligheten selv i en travel hverdag.**
  = They took time to nurture their love **even in** a busy everyday life.

### A useful pattern

**selv + preposition phrase** often means **“even + ...”**:

* **selv i Norge** = even in Norway
* **selv på vinteren** = even in winter
* **selv uten penger** = even without money
* **selv etter mange år** = even after many years
* **selv med lite tid** = even with little time

So in your sentence, **selv** emphasizes the surprising/unexpected circumstance: **a busy everyday life**.
