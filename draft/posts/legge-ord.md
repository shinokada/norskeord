Yes. Your intuition is good, but there is an important distinction:

**planla** is not really *plan + legge* as a modern compound. It is the **past tense of *planlegge***:

* **planlegge** = to plan
* **planla** = planned
* **har planlagt** = have planned

Here, **-legge** is related to **legge** (“to put/place”), and Norwegian has many verbs ending in **-legge**. In the past tense, these often change **-legge → -la**.

### Common examples

| Infinitive        | Meaning                        | Past tense     | Literally / originally |
| ----------------- | ------------------------------ | -------------- | ---------------------- |
| **planlegge**     | to plan                        | **planla**     | put a plan             |
| **anlegge**       | to establish, construct        | **anla**       | put/build              |
| **fastlegge**     | to determine, establish        | **fastla**     | put firmly             |
| **foreslå?**      | to propose                     | **foreslo**    | ❌ not -legge           |
| **tilrettelegge** | to facilitate, arrange         | **tilrettela** | arrange/put in place   |
| **tilpasse?**     | to adapt                       | **tilpasset**  | ❌ not -legge           |
| **nedlegge**      | to close/discontinue; lay down | **nedla**      | lay down               |
| **opplegge**      | to impose/levy                 | **oppla**      | lay upon               |
| **pålegge**       | to impose, require             | **påla**       | lay upon               |
| **ilegge**        | to impose (tax/fee/penalty)    | **ila**        | lay upon               |
| **underlegge**    | to subject to                  | **underla**    | lay under              |
| **overlegge**     | to discuss/consider            | **overla**     | lay over/to someone    |

The particularly useful pattern is:

> **-legge → -la → -lagt**

For example:

* **planlegge → planla → planlagt**
* **pålegge → påla → pålagt**
* **nedlegge → nedla → nedlagt**
* **fastlegge → fastla → fastlagt**
* **tilrettelegge → tilrettela → tilrettelagt**

### Why does *legge* become *la*?

This comes from the irregular verb **legge** itself:

> **å legge → la → har lagt**
> to put → put → have put

So when **legge** occurs as the second part of a compound verb, the past-tense pattern is often preserved:

> **plan + legge → planlegge**
> **plan + la → planla**

This is a very useful pattern to recognize when learning Norwegian vocabulary.

### A few especially common ones

**pålegge** is worth knowing:

> Arbeidsgiveren **påla** ham å jobbe overtid.
> The employer **required** him to work overtime.

> De har **pålagt** ham en bot.
> They have **imposed** a fine on him.

**nedlegge**:

> De **nedla** fabrikken.
> They closed the factory.

> Fabrikken er **nedlagt**.
> The factory has been closed.

**fastlegge**:

> Vi **fastla** datoen i går.
> We determined the date yesterday.

> Datoen er **fastlagt**.
> The date has been determined.

So when you see a Norwegian verb ending in **-legge**, it's often useful to ask yourself:

**“What happens to *legge* in the past tense?” → *la***

There are some exceptions and verbs where the meaning has become quite specialized, but this is a very productive pattern to recognize.
