


In Norwegian, **oppføre** and **opptre** can both relate to **behavior**, but they are used differently.

### 1. **oppføre seg** = to behave

**oppføre seg** is mainly about **how someone behaves**.

* **Han oppfører seg bra.**
  = He behaves well.
* **Hun oppfører seg merkelig.**
  = She behaves strangely.
* **Du må oppføre deg!**
  = You must behave!

It is normally used reflexively: **oppføre seg**.

Think: **“How does this person conduct themselves?”**

---

### 2. **opptre** = to act / appear / perform

**opptre** has several meanings, but importantly it can mean **to act or conduct oneself in a particular way**, often in a more formal context.

* **Han opptrådte profesjonelt.**
  = He acted/conducted himself professionally.
* **Hun opptrådte rolig under møtet.**
  = She remained/acted calmly during the meeting.
* **Politiet opptrådte korrekt.**
  = The police acted properly.

It also means **to perform**, especially on stage:

* **Bandet opptrer på en scene.**
  = The band performs on a stage.
* **Hun skal opptre på konserten.**
  = She is going to perform at the concert.

---

### The key difference

| Norwegian       | Main meaning        | Example                       |
| --------------- | ------------------- | ----------------------------- |
| **oppføre seg** | behave              | Han **oppfører seg** dårlig.  |
| **opptre**      | act/conduct oneself | Han **opptrer** profesjonelt. |
| **opptre**      | perform             | Hun **opptrer** på scenen.    |

A useful way to remember it:

> **oppføre seg** → everyday **behavior**
> **opptre** → **how someone acts in a situation** or **performs**

For example, these are both possible:

**Han oppfører seg dårlig overfor kollegene.**
→ He behaves badly toward his colleagues.
This focuses on his **behavior**.

**Han opptrer dårlig overfor kollegene.**
→ He acts poorly toward his colleagues.
This sounds somewhat more **formal/judgmental**, focusing on how he **conducts himself** in that situation.

Also, be careful with **oppføre** without *seg*: **å oppføre noe** can mean **to put on/stage something**, especially a play:

* **De skal oppføre et teaterstykke.**
  = They are going to stage/perform a play.

So the patterns are:

**å oppføre seg** → behave
**å opptre** → act / perform
**å oppføre et teaterstykke** → stage a play

Yes. **å arte seg** can mean **“to behave”**, but it is different from **å oppføre seg**.

### **å arte seg**

**Å arte seg** means **to behave / turn out / develop**, and it is often used to describe **how a situation, condition, or person manifests itself**.

Examples:

* **Hvordan arter sykdommen seg?**
  = How does the illness manifest itself?
* **Problemet arter seg på forskjellige måter.**
  = The problem manifests itself in different ways.
* **Han arter seg som en idiot.**
  = He behaves like an idiot.

However, for ordinary **“behave”**, use **oppføre seg**:

* **Barnet oppfører seg dårlig.**
  = The child behaves badly. ✅
* **Barnet arter seg dårlig.**
  = The child behaves badly. ⚠️ Unusual/unnatural in this context.

### Comparing the three

| Norwegian       | English                                 | Typical use                                      |
| --------------- | --------------------------------------- | ------------------------------------------------ |
| **oppføre seg** | to behave                               | someone's behavior                               |
| **opptre**      | to act / conduct oneself                | behavior in a particular situation; also perform |
| **arte seg**    | to manifest itself / turn out / develop | how something appears or develops                |

A very useful expression is:

**Hvordan arter det seg?**
= **How does it manifest itself? / What is it like? / How does it present itself?**

For example:

> **Hvordan arter problemet seg?**
> How does the problem manifest itself?

So although **arte seg** can sometimes be translated as **“to behave,”** I would **not** treat *arte seg = å oppføre seg* as a general rule. It is closer to **“to manifest itself / turn out / develop in a particular way.”**
