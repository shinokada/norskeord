It is common to mix **two different rules**, which is very understandable:

1. **V2 / inversion** (verb before subject)
2. **Placement of _ikke_** in main vs subordinate clauses

They’re related, but not the same thing.

## 1) Hovedsetning (main clause): **ikke usually comes after the finite verb**

Example:

> **Ida gjorde ikke mye husarbeid.**
> (subject + verb + ikke)

If another element comes first, you get inversion:

> **Hjemme hos foreldrene gjorde ikke Ida mye husarbeid.**

Structure:

- **Hjemme hos foreldrene** = first element
- **gjorde** = finite verb (must be 2nd)
- **ikke**
- **Ida** = subject

So:

> **X + verb + ikke + subject**

This is normal in Norwegian.

More examples:

> **I går kom ikke Per.**
> **På jobb snakker ikke hun norsk.**

---

## 2) Leddsetning (subordinate clause): **ikke comes before the verb**

Example:

> Jeg vet at **Ida ikke gjorde mye husarbeid**.

Structure:

- subject = Ida
- **ikke**
- verb = gjorde

So:

> **subject + ikke + verb**

Examples:

> fordi hun **ikke kommer**
> at jeg **ikke vet**
> hvis de **ikke har tid**

This is probably the rule you were thinking of.

---

## Compare side by side

**Hovedsetning:**

> Ida **gjorde ikke** mye husarbeid.

**Leddsetning:**

> ...at Ida **ikke gjorde** mye husarbeid.

That’s the key contrast:

- **main clause → verb + ikke**
- **subordinate clause → ikke + verb**

So your intuition about **ikke before verb in leddsetning** was **correct** — it just applies to a different rule than the sentence you asked about.
