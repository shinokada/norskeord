Yes — **vil** and **ønsker** can both translate as “want to,” but they are used differently.

### 1. **vil** = want to / intend to

**Vil** is the most common and natural word when you simply say what you want or intend to do.

* **Jeg vil spise.** = I want to eat.
* **Jeg vil lære norsk.** = I want to learn Norwegian.
* **Jeg vil reise til Japan.** = I want to travel to Japan.
* **Jeg vil ikke gå.** = I don't want to go.

It can also mean **“will”** or express a strong intention:

* **Jeg vil gjøre det.** = I will do it.
* **Hun vil bli lege.** = She wants to become a doctor.

So **vil + infinitive** is extremely common.

---

### 2. **ønsker** = wish / would like / desire

**Ønsker** sounds more like **“wish,” “would like,” or “desire.”** It is often more polite or formal.

* **Jeg ønsker å lære norsk.**
  = I would like to learn Norwegian.

* **Jeg ønsker å bestille et rom.**
  = I would like to book a room.

* **Jeg ønsker deg en god dag.**
  = I wish you a good day.

Notice that **ønsker** normally uses **å + infinitive**, whereas **vil** does **not**:

> **Jeg vil lære norsk.** ✅
> **Jeg ønsker å lære norsk.** ✅
> **Jeg vil å lære norsk.** ❌

---

### 3. Compare them directly

| Norwegian                         | Natural English                  | Feeling                      |
| --------------------------------- | -------------------------------- | ---------------------------- |
| **Jeg vil ha kaffe.**             | I want coffee.                   | direct, ordinary             |
| **Jeg ønsker kaffe.**             | I would like coffee.             | more formal/polite           |
| **Jeg vil kjøpe en bil.**         | I want to buy a car.             | intention/desire             |
| **Jeg ønsker å kjøpe en bil.**    | I would like to buy a car.       | wish/desire, somewhat formal |
| **Jeg vil reise til Japan.**      | I want to travel to Japan.       | normal                       |
| **Jeg ønsker å reise til Japan.** | I would like to travel to Japan. | more formal/deliberate       |

For everyday conversation, **vil** is usually the natural choice.

### 4. One important difference: **vil** can mean “will”

This is where **vil** is broader than English *want*.

**Jeg vil hjelpe deg.** can mean:

* “I want to help you.”
* “I will help you.”

The context determines the meaning.

But **Jeg ønsker å hjelpe deg** clearly means something like:

> “I wish/would like to help you.”

It doesn't normally have the same “I will” meaning.

### A useful rule

Think of them like this:

**vil + infinitive** → **want to / intend to / will**

**ønske + å + infinitive** → **wish to / would like to**

So if you're simply saying **“I want to…”**, start with **jeg vil …**.
