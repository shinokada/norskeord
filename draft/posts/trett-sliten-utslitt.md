Yes — Norwegian has several words that can mean “tired,” but they are used in different situations and with different feelings.

Here are the most common ones:

---

## 1. **trøtt** = sleepy / tired

This is the most basic and neutral word.

Usually about:

* needing sleep
* low energy
* feeling tired in general

### Examples

* Jeg er trøtt.
  = I’m tired.

* Jeg er så trøtt i dag.
  = I’m so tired today.

* Barna er trøtte.
  = The children are sleepy/tired.

### Important

“Trøtt” often means **sleepy**, not only exhausted.

---

## 2. **sliten** = worn out / exhausted

This is stronger than *trøtt*.

It means:

* physically exhausted
* mentally drained
* “worn down”

### Examples

* Jeg er helt sliten etter jobb.
  = I’m completely exhausted after work.

* Hun ser sliten ut.
  = She looks worn out.

* Etter maratonet var han veldig sliten.
  = After the marathon he was very exhausted.

### Difference from *trøtt*

* *trøtt* → need sleep
* *sliten* → exhausted from effort/stress

You can be:

* trøtt without being sliten
* sliten without being trøtt

Example:

* Jeg er sliten, men ikke trøtt ennå.
  = I’m exhausted, but not sleepy yet.

---

## 3. **utslitt** = completely exhausted / burned out

Even stronger.

Literally “worn out.”

Used for:

* extreme exhaustion
* no energy left
* also objects that are worn out

### Examples

* Jeg er helt utslitt.
  = I’m completely exhausted.

* Skoene er utslitte.
  = The shoes are worn out.

This is stronger and more dramatic than *sliten*.

---

## 4. **utmattet** = exhausted / fatigued

More formal or literary.

Often:

* medical
* emotional
* serious fatigue

### Examples

* Hun var fysisk og psykisk utmattet.
  = She was physically and mentally exhausted.

* Pasienten følte seg utmattet.
  = The patient felt fatigued.

Less common in everyday casual speech than *sliten*.

---

## 5. **dønn sliten / kjempesliten / veldig sliten**

These are intensifiers.

### Examples

* Jeg er dønn sliten.
  = I’m dead tired.

* Jeg er kjempesliten.
  = I’m super exhausted.

Very common in spoken Norwegian.

---

## 6. **lei** (sometimes)

Not physically tired.

Means:

* fed up
* tired of something emotionally

### Examples

* Jeg er lei av vinteren.
  = I’m tired of winter.

* Hun er lei av å vente.
  = She’s tired of waiting.

This is closer to:

* “fed up”
* “sick of”

---

# Quick comparison

| Norwegian | Meaning              | Strength      | Typical feeling    |
| --------- | -------------------- | ------------- | ------------------ |
| trøtt     | tired/sleepy         | mild          | need sleep         |
| sliten    | exhausted            | medium        | worn out           |
| utslitt   | completely exhausted | very strong   | no energy left     |
| utmattet  | fatigued/exhausted   | formal/strong | serious exhaustion |
| lei       | tired of/fed up      | emotional     | annoyance          |

---

# Natural combinations

### Very common:

* trøtt og sliten
  = tired and exhausted

### Example:

* Jeg er trøtt og sliten etter reisen.
  = I’m tired and exhausted after the trip.

Norwegians often use both together.

---

# One subtle point

## “Jeg er trøtt.”

Usually sounds temporary and normal.

## “Jeg er sliten.”

Can sometimes imply:

* stress
* hard work
* emotional burden

So *sliten* can feel heavier emotionally.
