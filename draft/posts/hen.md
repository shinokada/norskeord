**“hen”** is a small word that causes a lot of confusion.

---

# 🧭 Sentence:

**Hun vet ikke hvor jeg er hen**

👉 = She doesn’t know where I am

---

# 🧠 What does **“hen”** mean here?

👉 **“hen” = direction or place (informal emphasis)**
It is used to make questions or indirect questions about **location or direction**.

But in modern Norwegian, it is often:

👉 **optional / stylistic / spoken emphasis**

---

# 📍 Break it down:

* **hvor** = where
* **jeg er** = I am
* **hen** = “at / to where (emphasis)”

So:

> hvor jeg er hen
> = where I am (literally: “where I am at/located”)

---

# ⚖️ Important: you can REMOVE “hen”

Both are correct:

* Hun vet ikke hvor jeg er
* Hun vet ikke hvor jeg er hen

👉 Same meaning

---

# 🧠 When is “hen” used?

Mainly in **spoken Norwegian**, especially:

### 1. Questions

* Hvor skal du hen? = Where are you going?
* Hvor er du hen? = Where are you?

### 2. Indirect questions (like your sentence)

* Hun vet ikke hvor jeg er hen

---

# 🚗 Think of it like:

👉 “Where are you *at*?”
👉 “Where are you going *to*?”

It adds a slight **directional feeling**

---

# ⚠️ Important note

* “hen” is **not necessary**
* It is more **spoken / informal**
* In writing, many people just skip it

---

# 🔥 Compare

### A (with hen)

* Hvor skal du hen? (very common spoken)

### B (without hen)

* Hvor skal du? (also correct, slightly more neutral)

---

# 🧠 Simple rule

* **hvor + verb** → basic question
* **hvor + hen** → spoken emphasis on place/direction
