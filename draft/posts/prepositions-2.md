👉 **i vs på vs om (time)**

I’ll make it as clear and practical as possible.

---

# 🧭 1. The core idea

Think of it like this:

- **i** → inside a time period
- **på** → specific / habitual time points
- **om** → future / “in (time)”

---

# 📍 2. “i” = inside a time period

Used for:

- days (some cases)
- months, years
- parts of the day

### Examples:

- **i dag** = today
- **i morgen** = tomorrow
- **i går** = yesterday
- **i april**, **i 2024**
- **i sommer**

👉 Think: _inside a time frame_

---

# 📍 3. “på” = specific or repeated times

Used for:

- days of the week
- parts of the day (more specific/habitual)
- events

### Examples:

- **på mandag**
- **på kvelden**
- **på morgenen**
- **på ferie**
- **på fest**

👉 Often = something scheduled or recurring

---

# ⚠️ Key contrast (very important)

- **i kveld** = tonight (general)
- **på kvelden** = in the evening (habit / general time)

---

# 📍 4. “om” = future (“in”)

Used for:

- something that will happen after some time

### Examples:

- Vi sees **om to dager**
- Jeg er ferdig **om en time**
- Hun kommer **om litt**

👉 Think:
➡️ “after X time from now”

---

# ⚖️ 5. Compare (this is where people struggle)

### A.

- Jeg reiser **i morgen**
  → fixed day (tomorrow)

### B.

- Jeg reiser **om to dager**
  → after two days from now

---

### A.

- Jeg trener **på kvelden**
  → habit

### B.

- Jeg trener **i kveld**
  → specific evening (today)

---

### A.

- Jeg bodde der **i tre år**
  → duration

### B.

- Jeg flyttet hit **for tre år siden**
  → past point

---

# 🔥 6. Common mistakes (important)

### ❌ Jeg kommer i to timer

✅ Jeg kommer **om to timer**

---

### ❌ Jeg trener i kvelden

✅ Jeg trener **i kveld** / **på kvelden**

---

### ❌ Jeg reiser på morgen

✅ Jeg reiser **i morgen**

---
