# SAAS is dead and Norskeord

**The "SaaS is dead" narrative is mostly about a specific type of SaaS** — generic productivity tools (project managers, note apps, CRMs) that AI can now replicate in seconds as a custom ChatGPT. That's a real threat for those. But norskeord is a different animal.

Here's my realistic assessment:

**What works in your favour**

*Niche specificity.* Norwegian language learning is a small, defined market. ChatGPT can answer a Norwegian grammar question, but it can't replace the habit loop of spaced repetition with FSRS, structured CEFR progression, and daily streaks. The value isn't the information — it's the system.

*Real content moat.* You have 18+ published blog posts, 40+ in draft, vocab curated across A1–C, grammar questions, Norskprøven prep, and a growing blog with proper SEO (JSON-LD, sitemaps). This took years and can't be AI-generated overnight by a competitor.

*The user you're targeting is real.* People preparing for Norskprøven, immigrants in Norway, people with Norwegian partners — they have concrete motivation and a clear end goal. That's a much better customer than "someone who vaguely wants to learn Spanish."

*Personal connection.* You have a native Norwegian speaker close to you. That's an editorial advantage that keeps the content authentic in ways a generic AI tool can't fake.

**Where I'd be realistic / cautious**

*Market size is a ceiling, not a bug.* Norwegian is spoken by ~5 million people natively. The learner pool is real but not huge — probably tens of thousands serious enough to pay. That's a sustainable lifestyle SaaS, not a venture-scale one. That's fine, but worth being clear-eyed about.

*Discovery is the hard problem.* The product quality looks solid from what I can see. The risk isn't "AI killed SaaS" — it's "nobody found it." SEO through the blog is the right bet, but it takes 12–18 months to compound. You're building that infrastructure now (GA4, GSC, canonical tags), which is correct.

*The Plus conversion funnel matters a lot.* You have free users with FSRS and paid users with grammar + extended limits. The onboarding work you're doing right now (slides, profile nudges, completion banners) is exactly the right lever — free users who don't understand what they're getting won't convert. This is the most important thing to get right in 2025.

*Competition is Duolingo, not another indie.* Duolingo has Norwegian and millions of users. But Duolingo is gamified entertainment, not serious exam prep. Your Norskprøven angle is a direct differentiator — someone failing their B1 test will pay for something that actually works.

**Bottom line**

norskeord isn't at risk from "SaaS is dead." It's at risk from the normal indie SaaS problems: slow organic growth, low conversion if onboarding is unclear, and the grind of maintaining a real product solo. The "SaaS is dead" crowd is mostly people who built generic tools and got disrupted by GPT wrappers. You built something specific, content-rich, and grounded in a real human need. That's a different situation entirely.

The realistic ceiling is a profitable, sustainable niche product — not a unicorn, but a real business. Whether it gets there depends almost entirely on distribution (SEO + word of mouth in expat/immigrant communities) and whether your onboarding flow closes the gap between "signed up" and "understands the value."
