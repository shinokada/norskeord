# How to increase traffic to norskeord.no

## Consistent traffic

The goal is to give Google signals that real people find your site useful.

**Write more blog posts regularly.** Your Språkhjørnet blog is already set up well. One post per week or two weeks targeting specific search queries ("Norwegian word order", "difference between også and heller", "how to prepare for Norskprøven A2") will compound over time. Each post is a new entry point from search.

**Post in Norwegian learner communities.** Share useful blog posts (not just the homepage) in places where your audience already hangs out
— r/norsk on Reddit

- Facebook groups like "Learning Norwegian"
- the Duolingo Norwegian forum
- Discord servers for language learners.

Link to a specific post that genuinely answers a question someone asked.

**Answer questions on Reddit and forums.** Find threads where people ask things your blog posts already answer, and reply helpfully with a link. This drives direct traffic _and_ earns backlinks.

**Cross-post content on social.** Even a simple "word of the week" post on Instagram or Twitter/X with a link to the relevant deck builds a habit of return visitors.

---

## External links (backlinks)

These are harder to get but matter more for ranking.

**Get listed on Norwegian learning resource pages.** Several sites maintain curated lists of Norwegian learning tools — exactly the kind of page your `/resources` page links to. Email the maintainers of sites like Lenoo, Lesnorsk, or language learning blogs and ask if they'd consider adding Norskeord. A short, genuine pitch works better than a template.

**Reach out to Norwegian teachers and tutors.** Teachers who run blogs or recommend tools to students are a natural fit. If someone who teaches Norwegian mentions Norskeord on their site, that's a high-quality backlink from a relevant domain.

**Submit to app directories and language learning lists.** Sites like Alternativeto.net, ProductHunt, and language-learning resource roundups (many exist on Medium, Substack, and personal blogs) accept submissions. These links are lower authority but they add up.

**Guest posts.** Offer to write a post for an existing Norwegian learning blog in exchange for a link back. Your blog posts on the V2 rule or sakte vs langsomt are already the right kind of content — they just need to exist on high-traffic sites that will link back.

**The Røde Kors and NTNU angle.** You already link to them from `/resources` and acknowledge Røde Kors in your `/about` page. Consider emailing their digital teams to let them know — sometimes they maintain a "useful tools for learners" page and will add a reciprocal link.

---

The honest reality is that backlinks from relevant, trusted domains (Norwegian university sites, government language resources, established language learning blogs) move the needle far more than dozens of low-quality directory links. Two or three good ones are worth more than fifty generic ones.
